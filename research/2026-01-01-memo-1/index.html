    Execution-Aware Microstructure Study

    Execution-Aware Microstructure Study

    Abstract

    Research question: How do intraday order-book dynamics in commodity futures affect fill quality and short-horizon price returns?

    Instrument universe: Liquid front-month contracts in crude oil and refined products (generic rolling identifiers; no proprietary symbols).

    Dataset(s): Synthetic order-book and trade-print data; clean adapter stubs exist for vendor level-1 and level-2 feeds.

    Primary result: Placeholder — summarise the direction and magnitude of the effect, e.g. queue-position imbalance predicts short-horizon mid-price moves at the X-second horizon with an information coefficient of Y.

    Key caveat: Placeholder — e.g. the effect is sensitive to session-open volatility and degrades during high-impact macro releases.

    Hypothesis & Market Intuition

    Describe why order-book imbalance (or another microstructure feature) should predict short-term price moves given the economics of commodity futures markets — inventory risk, market-maker inventory cycles, informed order flow. Also discuss the mechanisms that would cause the effect to disappear: crowding, latency arms race, regime shifts.

    Data

    Sources: Synthetic top-of-book snapshots (1-second bars) and trade prints. Vendor adapters (e.g. Refinitiv Tick History, CME DataMine) are stubbed with the same interface — swap in real credentials to replicate on live data.

    Sampling: Intraday, restricted to exchange hours. Session-open and close periods flagged separately to avoid microstructure noise.

    Known issues:

    • Synthetic data does not capture correlated spread widening during macro events — production data would require event masking.
    • Timestamp alignment between trade prints and book snapshots requires sub-second precision; misalignment is a common source of look-ahead bias.
    • Survivorship: only front-month contracts included; roll mechanics handled by generic continuation series.

    Methodology

    Feature construction: Order-book imbalance (bid volume / (bid volume + ask volume) at top N levels), trade arrival rate, spread, and mid-price velocity. All features computed using only information available at time t, with no forward-fill that crosses the label window.

    Model: Logistic regression and a shallow gradient-boosted tree for comparison. Linear model preferred for interpretability and resistance to overfitting on short samples.

    Validation design:

    • Walk-forward expanding window — training set grows chronologically; test set is always future data.
    • Embargo period of 5 minutes between training and test windows to prevent label-bleed from overlapping returns.
    • Hyperparameter selection uses inner-fold CV on the training set only; never touching the test set.

    Results

    Fill in once experiments are run. Minimum reporting requirements:

    • Information coefficient (IC) and IC t-statistic — in-sample and out-of-sample separately.
    • Hit rate (directional accuracy) vs. cost-adjusted hit rate.
    • Returns attribution: time-of-day, contract, volatility regime.
    • Turnover and a simple linear cost model sensitivity (e.g. “effect decays to zero at X bps round-trip”).

    Robustness & Failure Analysis

    Required section — describe at least three stress tests:

    1. Parameter stability: How sensitive are results to the imbalance window length or the number of book levels?
    2. Sub-period stability: Does the effect hold in both trending and mean-reverting regimes?
    3. High-volatility degradation: The effect should weaken during macro releases — quantify by how much.
    4. When it fails: The most credible finding is a clear articulation of the conditions under which the strategy loses money.

    Implementation Notes

    Latency: Effective use of order-book signals requires sub-100ms execution latency. The methodology demonstration here is not latency-constrained (uses bar data), but a live system would need co-location and direct market access.

    Data freshness: Book snapshots must arrive without queuing delays; staleness of even 1–2 seconds materially changes the signal.

    Explicitly excluded: Proprietary parameterisations, live venue selection logic, and any firm-specific execution data.
