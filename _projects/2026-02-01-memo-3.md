## Abstract

**Research question:** How can alpha forecasts be translated into a portfolio that balances expected return, risk, and turnover? Does a simple risk-constrained optimiser add value over naive signal weighting?

**Instrument universe:** Synthetic cross-section of 50 instruments representative of equities, bonds, and commodities. No proprietary identifiers — all parameterised as generic assets with synthetic factor loadings.

**Dataset(s):** Synthetic returns and factor data generated from a calibrated factor model; vendor-data adapter stubs included with identical interfaces.

## Data

**Universe construction:** Synthetic cross-section with configurable factor exposures (market, value, momentum, commodity carry). Covariance matrix estimated via shrinkage (Ledoit-Wolf) to reduce estimation noise.

**Factor data:** Synthetic factor returns calibrated to realistic historical volatility and correlation structure. Vendor-ready adapters are stubbed for Barra-style factor data.

**Known issues:**
- Estimation error in covariance matrices is the dominant source of optimiser instability; this is explicitly modelled and tested.
- Survivorship bias: a live universe should include instruments that were delisted during the sample period; this is simulated by random dropout.
- Look-ahead in universe construction: the eligible universe is defined using only information available at the rebalance date.

---

## Methodology

**Optimisation:** Mean-variance with a linear transaction cost penalty (MVO-TC), and risk parity as a benchmark. The MVO-TC formulation is:

$$\max_w \; w^\top \hat{\mu} - \frac{\lambda}{2} w^\top \hat{\Sigma} w - \gamma \|w - w_{t-1}\|_1$$

where $$\hat{\mu}$$ is the alpha forecast vector, $$\hat{\Sigma}$$ is the shrunk covariance, $$\lambda$$ controls risk aversion, and $$\gamma$$ penalises turnover.

**Constraints:** Long-only (or long-short with leverage cap), maximum single-name weight, sector neutrality (optional).

**Validation:** Expanding-window walk-forward with covariance and alpha re-estimated each period. Parameters ($$\lambda$$, $$\gamma$$) are fixed from an initial training window and not re-tuned during the test period.

## Implementation Notes

**Computational requirements:** Quadratic programming scales well to a few hundred instruments. Beyond ~500 instruments, solver choice and warm-starting matter; document the solver used (e.g. CVXPY with OSQP).

**Risk model dependency:** The quality of the optimised portfolio is bounded by the quality of the risk model. A mis-specified covariance can make the "optimised" portfolio worse than naïve weighting.

**Live integration:** In production, this module would receive alpha forecasts and a fresh covariance estimate at each rebalance, output target weights, and feed into an execution management system for order generation.

**Explicitly excluded:** Live execution logic, actual position data, and any firm-specific risk model parameterisations.
